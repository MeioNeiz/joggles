# The scroll-loop gap: the device adds a screen (track 16, 2026-08-10/11)

**The device appends about 24 blank columns to a scrolling type 1 save, and the
preview was not showing them.** That is the mismatch Jacob reported and it was ours,
in one constant in `app/src/Preview.tsx`. What is not settled is whether the device's
24 columns arrive always or only once the save has been restored from flash, because
one earlier observation says a loop can be perfectly seamless. Resume state:
`.claude/locks/track-16`.

## Read this first: the one observation left

Two looks at the panel, no connection for the first, no flash for either. The store
already holds content this works on.

1. **Power-cycle `GLASSES-125B37`** (2s button hold, then on), then get it scrolling
   again and watch the gap between repeats.
2. **Re-save the same content in the same session** (any save from the app, or
   `bun run packages/cli/src/loopgap.ts run --yes` for the solid block) and watch
   again **without** power-cycling.

*Corrected 2026-08-12 by review 16, off a fresh decode of the same log: this said the
store holds the 27-column word of 07:48, and it has held four saves since. **Resident
now is a 24-column word committed 2026-08-12 01:04:31, scrolling `MODE 02 01` at
`SPEED 0x41`**, and its own bitmap carries a 10-column blank run, which the eye will
add to whatever the device contributes. Look 1 works on it as it stands, but the
cleaner subject is already proven to exist: the **240-column loop of 2026-08-11
23:46:34 has a longest blank run of 0**, so every dark column seen on it is the
device's and nothing is being subtracted by eye. Re-saving that loop is look 2 and
costs the one commit look 2 was always going to cost.*

| Look 1 (restored) | Look 2 (same session) | Reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ~one screen of dark | seamless, no dark | the 24 is a **restore** effect. Keep `LOOP = 'panel'` in `Preview.tsx` and say so; the in-session seamless loop is real but temporary |
| ~one screen of dark | ~one screen of dark | the 24 is **unconditional**, and the 2026-08-10 solid-block reading was wrong. Simplest outcome: `LOOP = 'panel'` is right always, and the seamless claim in `effects.ts` is wrong in every state |
| seamless | seamless | the 24 belongs to something neither look reproduces, and every magnitude below is suspect. Start again from the wire log |

The marker to watch for is whether the content **completely** leaves the panel. A
blank run of 24 in a 24-column window is fully dark for exactly one column step, so
"it goes properly empty, briefly" means 24 and "it stays empty for a beat" means more.

## The observation, and the arithmetic

Jacob, live, 2026-08-11, on the 07:48 content:

- the preview shows **not even one pixel** of gap between the last pixel of the word
  and the first pixel of the next repeat;
- the glasses show **a full screen width** of gap, the word completely leaving the
  panel before the next arrives;
- his judgement: the glasses' behaviour "is kinda how it should be i think".

| Model | Blank run predicted for 27 uploaded columns | Fits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| loop = N, the bare upload | 0 | no: the panel plainly gaps |
| loop = N + 24 | 24, one screen | **yes, exactly** |
| loop = N + 48 (a bracket each side) | 48, two screens | no: two screens is not one |
| loop = the whole 1536-byte store | ~700, minutes of dark | no |

*Corrected: an earlier version of this file predicted N + 48 and called one screen of
gap a refutation of the bracket altogether. Both were wrong. The record does hold 24
blank columns each side, and the walk meets one of them.* `DATS` zeroes the store and
starts type 1's counter at byte 48, `DATCP` records `ncols = N + 48` (`abs 0x1833e`),
so the content sits at store column 24. A scroll that resumes where the content starts
and wraps at `ncols` walks `N + 24` and never reaches the lead-in. *derived*, and it
is the only mechanism proposed that produces 24 rather than 0 or 48.

The preview half needs no mechanism at all: it walked `viewport.scrollOffsets` over
the bitmap, which for a 27-column gapless bitmap is 27 frames with no dark one in
them. Both halves of the mismatch are now accounted for.

**The original complaint is consistent too.** The 22:17 save was 27 columns of word
plus a 24-column client gap; under `N + 24` its panel gap is 48, two screen widths,
which is the "as if space for the max looping length of text" that started this.

## The supporting observation this file was missing: direction

*Added 2026-08-12 by review 16. It was recorded the same evening as the measurement
above, in another file, and nothing here or in the docblocks that point here cited it,
so the evidence read as one measurement against one contrary reading when it was
already two against one.*

**`research/vendor-app-protocol.md`, "Both scroll directions gap", 2026-08-11.** A type
1 loop scrolled both ways, Jacob by eye on the app's Effects screen: both directions
show dead space, neither is seamless, and **one direction shows it at the beginning of
the pass**. Read against the record's `[24 blank][content][24 blank]`, direction selects
**which** bracket a pass walks, not **how many**, which predicts one panel width either
way. That is a second, independent observation of the same one bracket, and it arrived
with a mechanism rather than only a magnitude.

It also predicts where in the pass the dark falls, which `viewport.marqueeOffsets`
reproduces without being told to: the one dark frame sits at step `N` of `N + 24` under
`dir` 0 and at step 24 under `dir` 1, so on a wide loop it reads as the end of a pass
one way and as the start the other. `viewport.test.ts` pins both.

**What it does not settle** is the size, which is the same open question: nobody has
measured whether the dead space is one panel width or two. `vendor-app-protocol.md`
says so in its own words, and it is the same look as look 1 at the top of this file.

**One discrepancy for the sitting, and it is about provenance rather than the model.**
That write-up describes a **wide** loop scrolled both ways from the same save. The app's
own wire log holds no such pair: its only `MODE 02 01` frames are 2026-08-12, against
24-column saves, and the 240-column loop of 2026-08-11 23:46 was only ever sent
`MODE 02 00`. Either the direction looks were taken on narrow content, or they were
taken from a build that was not writing this log (an `expo export` ran at 00:28). Worth
one line of confirmation before the observation is leaned on for width.

## The contrary observation, unexplained

2026-08-10, `loopgap.ts run --yes`: a solid 32x9 type 1 block, `SPEED 5`, `MODE 02
00`, disconnected, watched by Jacob. **It looped seamlessly, no dark pass at all**
(*verified*, one run, same session as the save, no power cycle recorded). Under
`N + 24` that block should have cleared the panel once per pass, which on a solid
block is unmissable.

The two observations differ in three ways and only one of them plausibly matters:
content (solid versus a word), speed (5 versus 65), and **whether the device had
restored the save from flash rather than still holding it from the upload**. The third
is the session/restore split, and the two looks at the top of this file separate it.
Nothing here should be built on the split until they have been done.

## The state change nobody commanded, also unexplained

*Historical as of 2026-08-12: this describes the panel on the morning of 2026-08-11,
and the redesign's sessions have driven it many times since, so the state it reports is
gone and only the question survives. Kept because the question is free to answer in the
sitting and nothing else has explained it.*

The panel was showing a **static** image of the word, clipped to the panel's 24
columns, having been sent `MODE 02 00` at 07:48 and nothing since. Two facts:

- **No `MODE 01` has ever been sent.** The decoded log now holds nine `MODE` frames,
  five `MODE 02 00` and four `MODE 02 01`, and not one `MODE 01`. So the scroll did not
  become static by our command (*verified*, from the log; the count was four when this
  was written and the conclusion is what survived re-decoding).
- **A 27-column word clipped to 24 loses its last glyph**, which is why the panel
  reads as one letter short. Consistent with the device displaying the store from the
  content's first column rather than from store column 0, which would be blank
  (*derived*, and worth confirming because it is the same look).

Three candidates, none settled: a power cycle, which loses `MODE` because it is RAM
while the store is flash and which would also make the scroll Jacob watched a restored
one; the on-board button, whose short press cycles built-in modes (*derived*, pin
unconfirmed); or a scroll that stops after some number of passes and rests on the
content. **What settles it:** power-cycle deliberately and see whether the panel comes
up static and one glyph short, then press the button once and see whether the display
changes. Both free, both in the same sitting as the two looks above.

**Why this matters more than a footnote:** the gap arithmetic above is built on the
column count of the resident save. That count is *verified* off the wire, so it
survives. What would not survive is any claim about a save nobody can point to on the
wire, which is why the lock's solid-block claim was checked rather than trusted.

## What landed in the code

Constants and docblocks in `core/src/dats.ts`, `content.ts` and `viewport.ts` carry
their own reasoning; the one thing the code does not say is which way the app is
pointed. `app/src/Preview.tsx` sets `LOOP = 'panel'`, and **that line was the bug**.
Flip it back to `'uploaded'` only if look 2 comes back seamless *and* the app is judged
better off previewing the saving session than the restored one, which is the state a
worn pair spends its life in.

## Is a genuinely seamless loop possible? Jacob's question, answered

**In the session that saves it, yes, and it has been seen**: the 32-column solid block
looped with no dark pass at all (*verified*, 2026-08-10). Nothing client-side is
needed for it beyond what is now the default, a bitmap with no trailing gap.

That run is the single observation this file cannot reconcile with the 27-column
measurement of 2026-08-11, which met a full screen width of blank. It is kept because it
is the contrary evidence and the reason the width is open, not because the two have been
squared: "yes, in the saving session" is one seamless pass on solid content at `SPEED` 5,
and the two looks at the top are what would make it a rule.

*Weakened 2026-08-12 by review 16, not withdrawn.* The direction observation above is a
second sighting of dead space, and if the loop it was taken on had not been power-cycled
then "seamless in the saving session" has a counter-example and the 2026-08-10 run is
the outlier rather than the rule. Whether it had been is not recorded, which is why this
paragraph is weakened rather than deleted. **Do not promise anyone a seamless loop on
stock in any state until look 2 has been taken.**

**Once the save has been restored from flash, on stock firmware, no** (*derived*, and
resting on the unexplained pair above). The 24 blank columns come from the record the
firmware writes, so no client value can subtract them: the choice a user has is one
screen width of gap or more, never less. The route to a seamless persistent loop is a
`JGX` sub-command that patches the wrap bound, which is cheap in principle - `ncols`
is a u16 in an 8-byte record at `0x3c800` - and blocked behind flashing, which is
blocked behind the SWD probe.

So the honest answer to give him: what he is looking at is the default from now on,
because it is what he asked for; seamless is real but currently lasts only until the
glasses are switched off; and making it permanent is a firmware job, not an app one.

## Consequences elsewhere

- `effects.ts` builds wide loops on the premise that the device wraps the buffer and
  the join is as visible as any other column. That holds for `uploaded` and fails for
  `panel`: a restored loop gains 24 blank columns, and `loopSeconds()` is short by
  24 columns of time, 1.9s at `SPEED` 100 and 6.3s at `SPEED` 0. The generators
  themselves need no change either way.
- Anything that sizes an upload against the 740-column ceiling is unaffected: the
  device's blank columns are in the record, not in the payload.
